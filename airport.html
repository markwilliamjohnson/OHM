Proposed executive pitch

From reacting to disruptions to learning how the whole airport responds

MAG already operates a complex network of aircraft, passengers, luggage, roads, stands, staff, equipment and services. A disruption in one flow can propagate through the others. Frontline teams make expert interventions, but their combined system-wide effects are difficult to reconstruct and compare.

Ord360 is a proposed decision-learning layer: it connects existing operational information, reconstructs connected events, records what was decided, tests plausible alternatives, and learns which responses are associated with better whole-system outcomes.

It does not replace airport operations control, safety processes or Airport Collaborative Decision Making (A-CDM). It extends their usefulness by concentrating on the consequences and learning generated by operational decisions.

What we propose testing

  1. Reconstruct a representative disruption across 4–6 linked operational flows.
  2. Build an explainable event-and-decision graph from existing data.
  3. Compare historical responses and run constrained agent-based counterfactuals.
  4. Capture operator feedback about what the model got right or wrong.
  5. Demonstrate how insights can transfer to subsequent disruptions and potentially across MAG airports.

What Ord360 adds

Operational memory — a searchable record of events, decisions, consequences and context.

Cross-flow insight — detect how a locally successful decision may shift the burden elsewhere.

Prediction with uncertainty — compare bounded response scenarios and show what is still unknown.

Organisational learning — convert experience into explicit, reviewable knowledge rather than replacing professional judgement.

Suggested first pilot

Scenario: baggage-handling disruption at one terminal during a busy departure wave.

Connect: bags, flights, passenger timing, gates, turnaround teams and apron resources.

Evaluate: delay minutes, queue-time profiles, recovery time, passenger impacts, resource trade-offs and forecast calibration.

Success criterion: show a repeatable improvement in decision support on held-out historical cases, with operations teams agreeing that the explanations are credible.

Important boundary

This demonstrator uses invented, qualitative relationships and synthetic agents. Its output cannot be used for live airport control or claims of real savings. A genuine pilot would need operational validation, information governance, data-sharing agreements, human approval, and a clear safety case.
