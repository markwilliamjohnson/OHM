The College for Occupational Health Transformation is a community associated with the University of Manchester’s Centre for Occupational and Environmental Health: practitioners, students, alumni, researchers and partners working to turn knowledge into healthier working lives.

Not a professional body. The College is a participatory space for inquiry, experimentation and collective action — extending the reach of academic research by connecting it directly with working practice.

Why a College?

A space for transformation, not just transmission.

Occupational health knowledge already exists in universities, clinics, workplaces, professional networks and the experience of workers themselves. The College creates an active structure in which those forms of knowledge can meet, challenge one another, and become changes in practice.

Practice is a source of knowledge

Students and practitioners bring live professional situations into the learning process — not as anecdotes, but as material for disciplined inquiry and redesign.

Research is a shared activity

Surveillance, epidemiology, workplace studies, service data and provider partnerships can become part of learning — while professional experience generates new research questions.

Learning should change systems

The test of education is not only what a participant can explain, but what becomes possible in clinics, organisations, sectors and policy because they can act differently.

≠

The College is not a regulator, credentialing organisation or representative professional body. It complements those institutions by concentrating on connection, experimentation, learning and transformation.

The transformation engine

Change moves in a spiral.

Research informs learning; learning is tuned to the situations practitioners face; practitioners change work; those changes reveal new evidence, constraints and questions. The College keeps that movement connected.

1 — Start with the situationWhat is happening in a clinic, workplace, organisation or sector? What matters to the people inside it?
2 — Listen before prescribingSurface aims, anxieties, constraints, uncertainty, capability and the practical consequences of current arrangements.
3 — Tune learningTeaching becomes responsive design: cases, methods, tools and AI are adapted to make participants more capable of acting.
4 — Connect live researchSurveillance, studies, provider relationships and practitioner inquiry supply evidence — and expose where evidence is missing.
5 — Act in professional practiceParticipants test new descriptions, interventions, services and forms of coordination in the systems where they already have agency.
6 — Create healthier workThe result sought is not merely compliance or treatment, but better conditions for health in the organisation of work itself.
7 — Build community and influenceWhat works can circulate across employers, providers, disciplines and public institutions — informing wider practice and policy.
8 — Return with better questionsEvery intervention generates new observations. The next cycle begins with richer evidence and a stronger community.
Research in motion

Evidence becomes more useful when practitioners can work with it.

The College can make Manchester’s research environment more permeable: researchers see live problems sooner; practitioners engage with methods and evidence more deeply; students learn through participation rather than only consumption.

A distributed college

The expertise is already in the room — and beyond it.

Many occupational health learners are experienced professionals with immediate access to consequential work situations. That makes education unusually capable of creating real-world change. The College keeps those people connected after a module, degree or project ends.

What the College does

Small structures that make useful collisions more likely.

The College can operate through lightweight, recurring formats that connect people who would not otherwise work on the same problem.

Occupational health should not only respond when work makes people ill. It should help society learn how to organise work so that health can develop.

The College exists to make that shift practical: from isolated expertise to connected inquiry; from knowledge transfer to knowledge creation; from individual interventions to the conditions of healthy work.

Join the network

Come with experience, questions, evidence — or a problem worth working on.

Membership is conceived as participation rather than status. The important question is not “what title do you hold?” but “what can we learn and change together?”
