Uncertainty and evidence anchors

Trials vary illness incidence, episode duration, intervention effectiveness, replacement costs and productivity. The starting illness parameters are illustrative and adjusted to produce roughly 4.4 all-cause absent days per worker in the reference setting. This is a modelling anchor, not a fitted sector model.

Employer counts and observed sector rates are context, not direct inputs to individual-case causal probabilities. A sector changes the prior risk profile; your sliders can override it.

Risk reduction is modelled, not established treatment effectiveness. Long-latency cancer and irreversible respiratory damage are underrepresented in a three-year cost horizon.

How to read this. Probabilities are conditional on the assumptions below. A positive net business effect means estimated avoided disruption and sick pay exceed the intervention's cost. It is not proof that an intervention works in a real workplace.

Model logic and interpretation

From work conditions to cases, absence and costs

For each trial, the app draws stress-related, MSD and exposure-related episodes in each of three years. Workload, autonomy, physical demand, hazardous exposure and contract pressure alter incidence. Control measures and early support reduce incidence or duration by uncertain amounts. Non-work illness days are included in capacity and sick-pay calculations but reported separately from work-related days.

Lost capacity creates pressure on remaining workers according to the cover and coordination settings; a portion feeds into next year's stress/MSD risk. Employer costs include sick pay, replacement/coordination and a share of uncovered output. Worker and public costs are separate proxies. Intervention costs include implementation and recurring support.

What the model cannot establish

Neither self-reported illness nor a sector rate proves work caused an individual case. The app does not estimate legal liability, individual diagnosis, occupational cancer mortality or a statistically identified return on investment. Compare assumptions and inspect whether gains depend on optimistic effectiveness or hidden cost transfers.

Evidence used for orientation
  • HSE GB work-related illness 2024/25: 1.9 million cases, including 964,000 stress/depression/anxiety and 511,000 MSD.
  • HSE sector rates: health/social work, public administration and education above all-industry average for self-reported work-related illness.
  • ONS 2025 sickness absence: 148.8 million days, 4.4 per worker, all causes.
  • HSE costs: £16.4bn for new work-related ill health in 2023/24, societal rather than employer cost.
  • Keep Britain Working technical note: broader £85bn employer impact model, with important uncertainties.
  • Business Perceptions Survey: general regulatory time and paperwork; not an OH-specific compliance cost.
