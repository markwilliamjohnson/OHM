How to read the geometry

guided interpretation
Grade 2 — an oriented plane

Each luminous disc is a bivector: e1∧e3, e2∧e6, or e4∧e5. Its direction is an orientation of area, not an ordinary arrow.

Grade 4 — two planes taken together

The connecting membranes stand for products of two independent bivectors. They are four-dimensional blades, shown here only by their incidence between plane-cells.

Grade 6 — all six directions

The breathing shell represents the pseudoscalar e1∧…∧e6: oriented six-dimensional volume. Its phase is shown by rotation and pulse.

A screen cannot literally embed six mutually independent directions in 3D. This view is a structural proxy: three separate 2-planes, their pairwise products, and their common grade-6 product.

Algebraic conventions used by the app

The engine uses the complexified Euclidean Clifford algebra Cl(6,0), with ek² = +1 and eaeb = −ebea for a ≠ b. The displayed imaginary unit is written j.

For the three pairs (1,3), (2,6), and (4,5), the ideal nilpotents are

a+k = ½(ea + j eb),    a−k = ½(ea − j eb),    (a±k)² = 0.

The ordered product a+1a+2a+3 a−1a−2a−3 gives the anti-idempotent convention V² = −V. Its scalar coefficient is −⅛. “Relative coefficients” divide every coefficient by that scalar, making the visible grade pattern 1 − j e13 − j e26 − j e45 + …, corresponding to the pattern on the photographed slide.

Different authors absorb signs and normalising constants into the definitions of the Witt basis, the vacuum, and the pseudoscalar. The app therefore exposes both V and P = −V and reports the polynomial residual numerically rather than hiding the convention.
